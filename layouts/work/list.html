The starting point

The website was only one part of the problem.

The series brings people together for seminars, tours, and expert insights around senior living. The public experience needed to make those events easy to understand and easy to join.

But behind each event was an ongoing operational process: publishing schedules, managing locations and partners, collecting attendee information, sending confirmations, tracking attendance, and preparing materials for the team on the ground.

We treated the website and the internal workflow as one connected product instead of two separate projects.

The process

From a visible experience to a complete workflow.

01

Understand the journey

Start with what people need to do.

We mapped the experience from the first visit to the event itself: finding the right seminar, understanding the details, registering an individual or spouse, receiving confirmation, and showing up with the right information.

02

Model the operation

Make the real-world relationships explicit.

The Django application models events, event series, locations, partners, attendees, registrations, news, FAQs, and other content so the team can manage the program in a way that matches how it actually operates.

03

Connect the handoffs

Move information where it is needed.

Registration includes duplicate-aware attendee lookup, phone normalization, validation, confirmation emails, and an authenticated service endpoint that lets n8n move registration information into the rest of the operation.

04

Support the people running it

Give the team visibility after launch.

A custom admin dashboard gives staff tools for event and partner content, attendee search and tagging, registration status, attendance tracking, and exports for sign-in sheets, nametags, attendee details, and analytics.

The result

Less manual work between interest and insight.

The finished product is a responsive public website powered by a production-oriented Django application. Visitors get a clearer path to the right event. Staff get one place to manage the information and relationships that keep the series moving.

That means the system is useful on both sides of the screen: it communicates the purpose of the series publicly and gives the team practical tools to operate it privately.
